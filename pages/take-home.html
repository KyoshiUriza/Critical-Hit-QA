Take-Home Simulator

The interview stage this site was missing: a written brief, a clock, a real app to break, and a submission that gets graded the way a hiring manager would read it.

How this differs from a bug hunt

Bug Bounty asks "how many can you find". A take-home asks something harder: did you stay in scope, pick the right thing to lead with, and write it so a stranger can act on it? Most candidates who fail this stage find the defects and lose on the write-up.

  • You get a brief from a named person, with a scope that is deliberately narrower than the app.
  • A clock runs. It does not stop you — but it records how long you took, because over-running a stated budget is itself a signal.
  • On submit you get a rubric: what a reviewer checks, and whether your submission met it.
  • The finished submission lands in your portfolio.

One honest note on grading. The objective parts — which defects you actually triggered, whether your report has steps, expected and actual — are checked automatically. Whether your writing is clear is not something this page can judge, so the rubric tells you what a human would look for rather than pretending to score it.
