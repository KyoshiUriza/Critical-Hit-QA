Windows, Tabs & Navigation

Eight behaviors that break naive automation: new tabs, a popup, a redirect, and a URL that changes without anything loading.

Why this exists. Every other practice app on this site lived on one page that never changed its URL, which made a whole category of automation unpractisable here. Waiting for a new tab, picking the right one out of several, following a redirect to where it actually lands, and telling a real navigation apart from a History push are all routine at work and all absent from most portfolios.

This lab is a target, not a quiz. It cannot see your Playwright script and does not pretend to grade one. Trigger a behavior, watch what the browser really does, then reveal the pattern — the reveal unlocks only after you have met the problem.

The rules worth carrying to work

  1. Wait and click together. Promise.all([context.waitForEvent('page'), click]). Clicking first and waiting second is a race, and it is the kind you lose one run in fifty.
  2. A new tab is a new page object. Your original page handle does not follow it. Most "my assertion passes but the tab is wrong" reports are this.
  3. Never pick a tab by index. context.pages() order is not a contract. Find it by URL or title.
  4. Wait for where you land, not the first hop. A redirect that used location.replace() is not even in history.
  5. A URL is a hint, not evidence. pushState moves the address bar with no load at all. Assert what the user can see.
  6. Close what you open. Tabs left behind leak into the next test, and a closed page throws on every call — read what you need before it goes.
The security one. A target="_blank" link without rel="noopener" gives the opened page a live handle to yours through window.opener, and it can navigate you elsewhere. Browsers now imply it; older clients do not. It is a legitimate finding, and this lab has one of each so you can see the difference rather than take it on trust.
