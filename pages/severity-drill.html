Severity & Priority Drill

Ten defects. Call the severity and the priority before you read the reasoning, and find out which way you lean.

Severity is what the defect does. Priority is when it gets fixed. They are two different questions answered by two different people, and interviewers probe the distinction constantly because collapsing them is how a bug queue stops meaning anything.

Severity is largely yours to judge: impact if it happens, whether there is a workaround, whether data is wrong. Priority belongs to the business: how many users, how visible, what else is in flight, what ships next week. Your job on priority is to supply the evidence, not to win the argument.

These are model answers, not facts. Grading treats a one-band disagreement as defensible on purpose. Two bands apart is a different thing, and it is what the report at the end counts.

The four questions that decide severity

  1. What is lost? Function, data, money, trust, time. Wrong data outranks a crash, because a crash is loud and wrong data is silent.
  2. Is there a workaround? A defect with a cheap workaround is not the same defect as one without, and the report should name the workaround and what it costs.
  3. Is it silent? A failure that tells the user it succeeded is worse than one that visibly fails — nobody reports what they do not notice.
  4. Is it systematic? One order out by a cent is a rounding nit. Two hundred a month that never reconcile is an audit finding.

And the four that decide priority

  1. How many users, how often? Bring the number. "Some users" loses to "0.2% of sessions, falling".
  2. Who are they? Two admins with a workaround and every visitor on a paid campaign are different answers to the same severity.
  3. What ships next? A regional formatting bug is P3 until the regional launch is nine days away.
  4. What does it cost to wait? Support volume, reconciliation work, legal exposure. This is the sentence that actually moves priority.
The mistake that costs credibility is filing everything high. It works once. After that your reports get discounted before they are read, and the cost lands on the day something genuinely is critical. Calibration is not caution — it is the thing that makes your urgent report believable.
