Cadence Calendar (buggy build)

Timezones and daylight saving. The hardest ordinary thing in software, and six defects that all ship for real.

The specification — An event is scheduled at a wall-clock time in a real timezone. A viewer in another zone sees the same instant converted to their own local time. An all-day event is a date, not a time, and shows on that date for everyone. A meeting booked for 60 minutes lasts 60 minutes. The agenda lists events in chronological order.

Every deviation from that paragraph is a defect. Three of the six only appear on two specific dates in 2026 — the preset buttons take you there.

What makes this category hard

  • Some wall-clock times do not exist. When clocks go forward, an hour is skipped. 02:30 on that date is not a time; every library resolves it to something, and none of them agree on what.
  • Some wall-clock times happen twice. When clocks go back, an hour repeats. "01:30" is two different instants an hour apart, and picking one silently is a decision the user should have made.
  • An offset is not a timezone. UTC-5 is an offset; America/New_York is a rule about which offset applies when. Storing the offset loses the rule, so a future event stored as -05:00 is wrong after March.
  • A date is not an instant. All-day events, birthdays and deadlines have no time. Giving them midnight in some zone is how a birthday lands on the wrong day for half your users.
  • Transitions differ by country. The US and the EU change on different weekends, so for two weeks a year the gap between New York and London is not what anyone's code assumes.
The test data worth remembering: the two transition dates in each zone you support, the day either side of them, and a date far enough in the future that the rules could change. Add a user in a zone with a half-hour offset — India, or Adelaide — and one in a zone with no DST at all. That is five values and it finds most of this.
